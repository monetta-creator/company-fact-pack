# Segment map

## Segment Structure and Definitions

Capital One organizes continuing operations into three reportable segments — Credit Card, Consumer Banking, and Commercial Banking — plus an Other category for activity not attributable to a segment, such as the corporate investment portfolio and asset/liability management performed by the centralized Corporate Treasury group [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2018-02-21_0000927628-18-000107#main:ITEM7]. As of the FY2025 10-K, the segments are defined as: Credit Card (domestic consumer card lending, personal loans, domestic small business card lending, and international card businesses in the U.K. and Canada); Consumer Banking (deposit gathering and lending for consumers and small businesses, national auto lending, and Global Payment Network services); and Commercial Banking (lending, deposit gathering, capital markets, and treasury management for commercial real estate and commercial/industrial customers, typically companies with $20 million to $2 billion in annual revenue) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM1]. The acquired Home Loan business is reported as discontinued operations and excluded from segment results [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM1]. The same filing notes structural seasonality: credit card purchase volume and receivables run highest around the winter holidays, while credit card net charge-off rates are typically highest in Q1 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM1].

Within Consumer Banking, the auto loan book is monitored by FICO score at origination and the retail banking book by delinquency status — this credit-quality framing is unchanged between the Q1 2026 and Q2 2026 10-Qs [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2026-05-07_0000927628-26-000048#main:ITEM1] [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2026-07-28_0000927628-26-000089#main:ITEM1].

## Reporting Methodology and Reclassifications

Segment results are reported on a continuing-operations basis and are built to approximate stand-alone business performance using internal allocation methodologies, principally funds transfer pricing (FTP) on a matched-maturity basis: business segments get a funds credit for sources of funds (e.g., deposits from Consumer and Commercial Banking) and a funds charge for use of funds, with total interest income and net fees attributed directly to the originating segment [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2016-02-25_0000927628-16-000140#main:ITEM8] [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2018-02-21_0000927628-18-000107#main:ITEM7]. Capital One states it periodically revises segment definitions or allocation methodology and recasts prior periods accordingly [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2018-02-21_0000927628-18-000107#main:ITEM7].

One documented recasting: in Q1 2019 the company changed how it allocates tax benefits on certain tax-advantaged investments within Commercial Banking, recasting prior periods. The change reduced previously reported Commercial Banking total net revenue by $26 million (three months) and $88 million (nine months) ended September 30, 2018, with an offsetting increase to Other and no consolidated impact [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2019-10-31_0000927628-19-000312#main:ITEM1]. The same recast reduced Commercial Banking revenue by $30 million for the three months ended March 31, 2018 [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2019-05-01_0000927628-19-000177#main:ITEM1], and by $32 million and $62 million for the three and six months ended June 30, 2018 [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2019-07-31_0000927628-19-000274#main:ITEM1]. This followed an earlier Q1 2018 change that began including tax benefits of losses on certain tax-advantaged investments in Commercial Banking on a taxable-equivalent basis, offset in Other [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2018-05-02_0000927628-18-000179#main:ITEM1].
