# Corporate history and lineage

## Corporate history and lineage

### Corporate form and founding
Capital One Financial Corporation is a Delaware corporation established in 1994, headquartered in McLean, Virginia [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2018-02-21_0000927628-18-000107#main:ITEM1]. This founding date and jurisdiction are repeated consistently across filings through the most recent 10-K on file, including FY2022 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2023-02-24_0000927628-23-000117#main:ITEM1], FY2023 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM1], FY2024 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2025-02-20_0000927628-25-000092#main:ITEM1], and FY2025 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM1]. The packet contains no filing describing the pre-1994 formation, spin-off from Signet Banking Corporation, or IPO — only the incorporation year and the residue of that lineage in later governance documents (see below).

### The Signet Banking Corporation reference and its 2023 removal
The one explicit textual trace of Capital One's pre-1994 lineage in this packet is a set of references to "Signet Banking Corporation" embedded in the company's Restated Certificate of Incorporation. In 2023, Capital One's board and shareholders acted to strip these references out. The 2023 proxy statement asked shareholders to approve "amendments to Capital One Financial Corporation's Restated Certificate of Incorporation to remove remaining supermajority voting requirements and references to Signet Banking Corporation" [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2023-03-22_0001193125-23-076700#main:ITEM1] [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2023-03-22_0001193125-23-076700#main:preamble]. The board unanimously recommended a "FOR" vote on this item [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2023-03-22_0001193125-23-076700#main:ITEM2], and the proxy card itself listed it as management item 2 [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2023-03-22_0001193125-23-076700#main:ITEM6]. The subsequent 8-K confirms shareholders approved the amendment at the May 4, 2023 annual meeting, alongside ratification of Ernst & Young LLP as auditor and adoption of the Seventh Amended and Restated 2004 Stock Incentive Plan [src:edgar-cof/8-K_2023-05-09_0000927628-23-000197#main]. The packet does not include the certificate text itself or any narrative explaining what Signet Banking Corporation was or its relationship to Capital One's founding — only that references to it existed in the charter as of early 2023 and were formally excised. This is a governance-cleanup event, not a substantive account of lineage, and should be flagged as such in any downstream brief.

### The North Fork acquisition (2006) and its long tail
The most substantively documented acquisition in the packet is the December 2006 acquisition of North Fork Bancorporation, Inc. This transaction brought two problem units into Capital One that were run off over the following decade-plus as discontinued operations: GreenPoint Mortgage Funding, Inc.'s wholesale mortgage origination business, and the manufactured housing operations of GreenPoint Credit, LLC (a GreenPoint subsidiary) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2017-02-23_0000927628-17-000115#main:ITEM8]. The manufactured housing book had actually been sold to a third party in 2004, before North Fork owned it, but Capital One inherited retained interests and obligations in it through the acquisition [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2016-08-01_0000927628-16-000200#main:ITEM1]. The wholesale mortgage origination unit itself was closed in Q3 2007 [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2016-11-03_0000927628-16-000228#main:ITEM1]. This discontinued-operations treatment persisted in quarterly and annual filings for over a decade: it appears in the Q1 2016 [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2016-05-04_0000927628-16-000160#main:ITEM2], Q2 2016 [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2016-08-01_0000927628-16-000200#main:ITEM1], Q3 2016 [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2016-11-03_0000927628-16-000228#main:ITEM1], FY2016 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2017-02-23_0000927628-17-000115#main:ITEM8], and Q3 2018 [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2018-11-01_0000927628-18-000317#main:ITEM1] reports. By Q3 2018, the residual size of this legacy was small: income from discontinued operations, net of tax, was $1 million in Q3 2018 versus a $30 million loss in Q3 2017, and $7 million versus $26 million loss for the nine months ended September 30 in 2018 vs. 2017, respectively; as of September 30, 2018, Capital One had "no significant continuing involvement" in the wholesale mortgage unit [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2018-11-01_0000927628-18-000317#main:ITEM1]. In Q4 2017, Capital One took on the obligation to exercise remaining clean-up calls on certain manufactured housing securitizations under an agreement with the third-party servicer [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2018-11-01_0000927628-18-000317#main:ITEM1]. The North Fork deal also generated PCI (purchased credit-impaired) loan accounting alongside later acquisitions (HFS in Q4 2015, ING Direct, Chevy Chase) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2017-02-23_0000927628-17-000115#main:ITEM7].

### Mortgage origination lineage: three acquired subsidiaries
The packet identifies a second, related lineage thread: Capital One's mortgage representation-and-warranty exposure traces to three acquired loan-originating subsidiaries — Capital One Home Loans, LLC (acquired February 2005), GreenPoint (acquired December 2006 via North Fork), and CCB (acquired February 2009, later merged into CONA) [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2016-05-04_0000927628-16-000160#main:ITEM2]. As of March 31, 2016, the aggregate representation-and-warranty reserve across all three entities was $613 million, up from $610 million at December 31, 2015 [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2016-05-04_0000927628-16-000160#main:ITEM2]. This confirms CCB→CONA as a distinct historical merger separate from the North Fork/GreenPoint lineage, though the packet gives no further detail on the CCB acquisition itself.

### Bank subsidiary consolidation: COBNA into CONA (2022)
A more recent structural simplification is documented directly: on October 1, 2022, Capital One completed the "Bank Merger," merging Capital One Bank (USA), National Association (COBNA) into Capital One, National Association (CONA), with CONA surviving [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2023-02-24_0000927628-23-000117#main:ITEM1]. Prior to this, filings described two "principal subsidiaries" — COBNA (credit/debit cards, lending, deposits) and CONA (broad banking products) — collectively "the Banks" [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2017-02-23_0000927628-17-000115#main:ITEM8 cites elsewhere; see 10-K_2018-02-21#ITEM1 and 10-K_2019-02-20#ITEM1]. Specifically, FY2017 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2018-02-21_0000927628-18-000107#main:ITEM1] and FY2018 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2019-02-20_0000927628-19-000093#main:ITEM1] 10-Ks both describe this two-bank structure. Post-merger, FY2022 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2023-02-24_0000927628-23-000117#main:ITEM1] and FY2023 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM1] 10-Ks describe CONA as the sole "principal operating subsidiary," with "the Bank" redefined to mean CONA after the merger and CONA-and-COBNA collectively before it.

### The Discover merger (2025)
The packet documents a major, recent structural event: on May 18, 2025 (the "Closing Date"), Discover Financial Services merged into Capital One and Discover Bank merged into CONA [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM1]. The FY2025 10-K frames this integration as coinciding with a repositioning of Capital One's business description, from a "diversified financial services holding company" to one operating "as a global payments provider and diversified financial institution" [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM1], and states Capital One became the largest credit card issuer in the U.S. by outstanding balance as of December 31, 2025 — up from third-largest as of FY2022–FY2024 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM1] versus [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2025-02-20_0000927628-25-000092#main:ITEM1] and [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM1].
