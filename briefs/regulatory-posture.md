# Regulatory posture

## Enforcement History: Consent Orders and Penalties

Capital One's most significant enforcement record centers on a 2020 OCC consent order against Capital One, N.A. covering compliance risk management, data governance, capital planning, and internal controls. The order required a board-level Compliance Committee (majority independent directors) that meets quarterly and reports progress to the Examiner-in-Charge within 45 days of quarter-end, plus a Comprehensive Action Plan combining a Consent Order Action Plan and Data Governance Plan, both subject to OCC "no supervisory objection" review [src:enforcement/occ-2020-aml-consent-order-cobna#main]. It mandated root-cause analysis of internal-control failures identified by internal audit or federal regulators "since 2017," enhanced capital-planning governance, and a training program covering front-line, compliance, and audit staff [src:enforcement/occ-2020-aml-consent-order-cobna#main]. The order was issued as a cease-and-desist order under 12 U.S.C. § 1818(b), (e), (h)(2), and (i), remaining in force until amended or terminated in writing by the OCC [src:enforcement/occ-2020-aml-consent-order-cobna#main].

Separately, per Capital One's own FY2023 10-K, the company entered consent orders with the Federal Reserve and OCC in August 2020 tied to the 2019 Cybersecurity Incident, paying a civil monetary penalty under the OCC agreement; both regulators have since terminated those orders. In January 2021, Capital One, N.A. paid a separate civil monetary penalty assessed by FinCEN for AML violations alleged to have occurred between 2008 and 2014 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM1A]. The packet does not specify whether the enforcement-corpus AML/BSA order is the same instrument as the FinCEN action or the terminated Fed/OCC cybersecurity orders — this is flagged below.

## Current Regulatory Priorities and Risk Framing

Across ten years of 10-K risk factors, Capital One's disclosed regulatory perimeter has both widened and sharpened in language. The FY2015 filing frames scrutiny broadly — comprehensive regulation and periodic examination by the Federal Reserve, SEC, OCC, FDIC and CFPB, with DOJ investigation exposure [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2016-02-25_0000927628-16-000140#main:ITEM1A]. FY2020 and FY2021 filings repeat near-identical language on formal enforcement actions and nonpublic supervisory findings restricting activities or M&A capacity [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM1A] [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2022-02-25_0000927628-22-000106#main:ITEM1A]. The FY2023 filing names specific priority areas — AML/sanctions, privacy/data security, service-provider use, fair lending, and technology-enabled activities — and explicitly cites the AML Act of 2020 as a driver of continued scrutiny [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM1A]. FY2024 adds AI to that list and introduces regulator language on "escalating consequences for banks that do not timely resolve open issues or have repeat issues," plus unfair-or-deceptive-practices exposure [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2025-02-20_0000927628-25-000092#main:ITEM1A]. The FY2025 filing (filed 2026-02-19) restates these priorities and adds a standalone risk factor citing the "structure of the credit card industry" and prosecutorial focus on banks as litigation/enforcement drivers, alongside new supervisory exposure discussed below [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM1A].

## Capital Regulation: Stress Capital Buffer Trajectory

Capital One's stress capital buffer (SCB) — recalibrated annually off Federal Reserve severely-adverse stress-test results, floored at 2.5% — has moved materially over the disclosed period: 2.5% for Oct 2021–Sept 2022 (min. CET1/Tier1/Total of 7.0%/8.5%/10.5%) [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2022-07-29_0000927628-22-000246#main:ITEM2]; 3.1% for Oct 2022–Sept 2023 (7.6%/9.1%/11.1%) [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2023-05-05_0000927628-23-000168#main:ITEM2] [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM7]; 4.8% for Oct 2023–Sept 2024 (9.3%/10.8%/12.8%); and 5.5% for Oct 2024–Sept 2025 (10.0%/11.5%/13.5%) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2025-02-20_0000927628-25-000092#main:ITEM7]. The 2025 stress test dropped the requirement to a final 4.5% for Oct 2025–Sept 2026 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM7]. On February 4, 2026, the Fed notified all participating firms it would hold SCB requirements at current levels pending public comment on its Stress Testing Transparency Proposal, meaning Capital One's 4.5% SCB is set to persist through September 30, 2027 absent further action [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2026-05-07_0000927628-26-000048#main:ITEM2]. The Bank entity (Capital One, N.A.) is not subject to the SCB rule; under OCC capital regulations its conservation buffer is fixed at 2.5% [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2025-02-20_0000927628-25-000092#main:ITEM7]. Underlying this framework, reported CET1 capital grew from $28.8B (2016) to $73.0B (2025) [src:edgar-cof/xbrl_companyfacts], though the packet does not connect this dollar growth to the ratio moves above (denominator/risk-weighted-asset figures are absent).

## Discover Acquisition: Inherited Regulatory Exposure

The Discover transaction imported standing regulatory matters. Discover operated under a 2020 CFPB consent order requiring a redress and compliance plan, at least $10 million in consumer redress, and a $25 million civil money penalty, plus a September 2023 FDIC consent order requiring an improved consumer compliance management system and enhanced board oversight [src:edgar-cof/S-4A_2024-07-26_0001193125-24-184991#main:PARTII]. Discover also disclosed an unresolved card-product-misclassification matter, an associated regulatory-penalty charge taken in Q2 2024, and a cooperating SEC investigation [src:edgar-cof/S-4A_2024-07-26_0001193125-24-184991#main:PARTII]. Capital One's FY2025 10-K confirms it "assumed the contingencies and liabilities of Discover," including ongoing litigation, government investigations, and enforcement actions tied to Discover's pre-Transaction conduct [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM1A].

## Emerging Supervisory Matter: OCC Debanking Review

In December 2025 the OCC issued preliminary findings from a supervisory review — conducted under Executive Order 14331, "Guaranteeing Fair Banking for All Americans" — of the nine largest OCC-regulated banks, including Capital One, N.A., examining whether those banks debanked or discriminated against customers based on political or religious beliefs or lawful business activities. The OCC states the review is ongoing [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM1A]. The packet does not include the content of the preliminary findings themselves.
