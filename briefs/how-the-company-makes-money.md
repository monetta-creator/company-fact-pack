# How the company makes money

## Business Model and Segments

Capital One operates as a diversified bank holding company organized into three reportable segments: Credit Card, Consumer Banking, and Commercial Banking. Credit Card covers domestic consumer and small-business card lending plus international card businesses in the United Kingdom and Canada; Consumer Banking covers deposit-gathering and lending for consumers and small businesses plus national auto lending; Commercial Banking covers lending, deposit-gathering, capital markets, and treasury management for commercial real estate and commercial/industrial customers, typically firms with annual revenues between $20 million and $2 billion [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM1]. Profitability across segments is shaped by customer usage and payment patterns, expected credit losses, marketing spend, and operating efficiency; credit card purchase volume and outstandings are seasonal, peaking around the winter holidays, while credit card net charge-off rates tend to run highest in the first quarter [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM1].

## Net Interest Income: The Core Revenue Engine

Net interest income — interest income (including certain fees) on loans, investment securities, and other interest-earning assets, less interest expense on deposits and wholesale borrowings — is the largest revenue component [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM7]. Credit card loans carry materially higher yields than the rest of the book: total credit card yield was 14.57% in Q1 2016 versus 6.18% for consumer banking and 3.37% for commercial banking loans in the same period [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2016-05-04_0000927628-16-000160#main:ITEM2], and the credit card yield rose to 14.67% for FY2016 and 15.21% for FY2017, versus consumer banking yields of 6.33%–6.66% and commercial banking yields of 3.47%–3.86% over the same years [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2017-02-23_0000927628-17-000115#main:ITEM7][src:edgar-cof/10-K_2018-02-21_0000927628-18-000107#main:ITEM7]. FY2023 net interest income growth was driven by higher average credit card loan balances and higher asset yields, partly offset by higher funding costs; period-end loans held for investment grew $8.1 billion to $320.5 billion, primarily from credit card growth, while average loans held for investment rose $19.3 billion to $311.5 billion [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM7]. Credit performance moved against the company that year: net charge-off rate rose 134 bps to 2.70%, 30+ day delinquency rose 78 bps to 3.99%, and the allowance for credit losses rose $2.1 billion to $15.3 billion (coverage ratio 4.77%) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM7]. Consolidated net interest income (XBRL) grew from $17.8 billion in FY2014 to $29.2 billion in FY2023, $31.2 billion in FY2024, and $42.9 billion in FY2025 [src:edgar-cof/xbrl_companyfacts].

## Non-Interest Income: Interchange and Fees

Non-interest income "primarily consists of interchange income net of rewards expense, service charges and other customer-related fees, and other non-interest income" including mortgage representation/warranty items, securities gains/losses, and derivative gains/losses outside hedge accounting [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2016-02-25_0000927628-16-000140#main:ITEM7]. Net interchange fees grew steadily: $2.573 billion (FY2017), $3.179 billion (FY2019), $4.606 billion (FY2022), $4.793 billion (FY2023), $4.882 billion (FY2024) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2018-02-21_0000927628-18-000107#main:ITEM7][src:edgar-cof/10-K_2020-02-20_0000927628-20-000102#main:ITEM7][src:edgar-cof/10-K_2025-02-20_0000927628-25-000092#main:ITEM7]. Service charges and other customer-related fees were $1.976 billion in FY2024, $1.667 billion in FY2023, and $1.625 billion in FY2022 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2025-02-20_0000927628-25-000092#main:ITEM7]. Total non-interest income rose from $4.777 billion (FY2017) to $5.253 billion (FY2019), driven in FY2018 by gains on exited-business sales (including the consumer home loan portfolio) and higher purchase-volume-driven interchange [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2019-02-20_0000927628-19-000093#main:ITEM7][src:edgar-cof/10-K_2020-02-20_0000927628-20-000102#main:ITEM7], and continued to $7.136 billion (FY2022), $7.546 billion (FY2023), and $7.904 billion (FY2024) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2025-02-20_0000927628-25-000092#main:ITEM7][src:edgar-cof/xbrl_companyfacts]. XBRL shows non-interest income reaching $10.556 billion in FY2025 [src:edgar-cof/xbrl_companyfacts].

## Funding Sources

Deposits are described consistently across filings as "the Company's primary source of funding," providing "a stable and relatively low cost of funds," supplemented by senior/subordinated notes, securitized debt obligations, brokered deposits, FHLB advances, and repo/fed funds borrowings to maintain "a diversified mix of wholesale funding sources" [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2016-02-25_0000927628-16-000140#main:ITEM7][src:edgar-cof/10-K_2018-02-21_0000927628-18-000107#main:ITEM7][src:edgar-cof/10-K_2019-02-20_0000927628-19-000093#main:ITEM7][src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2017-11-01_0000927628-17-000279#main:ITEM2][src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2018-05-02_0000927628-18-000179#main:ITEM2]. As of December 31, 2023, total funding sources were $398.269 billion: deposits $348.413 billion (87%), securitized debt obligations $18.043 billion (5%), other debt $31.813 billion (8%) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM7]. Within deposits, Consumer Banking supplied $296.171 billion (74% of total, up from $270.592 billion/71% in FY2022), Commercial Banking $32.712 billion (8%, down from $40.808 billion/11%), and Other $19.530 billion (5%) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM7]. XBRL total-deposit figures show growth from $305.4 billion (2020-12-31) to $475.8 billion (2025-12-31) and $489.1 billion (2026-03-31) [src:edgar-cof/xbrl_companyfacts].

## Credit Losses and Provisioning

The provision for credit losses is "driven by charge-offs, changes to allowance for loan and lease losses, and changes to unfunded lending commitments" [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2016-02-25_0000927628-16-000140#main:ITEM7]. It was $5.9 billion (FY2018, 25.6% of NII), $7.6 billion (FY2017, 33.6%), and $6.5 billion (FY2016, 30.9%) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2019-02-20_0000927628-19-000093#main:ITEM7]. The allowance methodology has three components — loans collectively evaluated for impairment, an asset-specific component for individually impaired loans, and a component for purchased-credit-impaired/acquired loans with post-acquisition cash-flow declines — each supplemented for modeling and process risk [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2016-02-25_0000927628-16-000140#main:ITEM8][src:edgar-cof/10-K_2020-02-20_0000927628-20-000102#main:ITEM8]. A parallel expected-credit-loss allowance applies to available-for-sale securities, capped at the amount by which amortized cost exceeds fair value, with securities in unrealized-gain positions excluded [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM8][src:edgar-cof/10-K_2023-02-24_0000927628-23-000117#main:ITEM8].

## Segment Economics

Credit Card is the dominant profit engine. FY2021 segment data: Credit Card generated $18.880 billion revenue (62% of total) and $7.758 billion net income (63% of total); Consumer Banking $9.002 billion revenue (29%)/$3.676 billion income (30%); Commercial Banking $3.301 billion revenue (11%)/$1.532 billion income (12%); consolidated totals were $30.435 billion revenue and $12.394 billion net income [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2022-02-25_0000927628-22-000106#main:ITEM7]. FY2023 detail: Credit Card net interest income $19.729 billion, non-interest income $5.940 billion, provision $8.651 billion, net income $3.457 billion, loans held for investment $154.547 billion; Consumer Banking net income $2.258 billion, deposits $296.171 billion; Commercial Banking net income $691 million, deposits $32.712 billion; consolidated net income $4.887 billion [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM8]. Credit Card segment net income has swung with the cycle: $2.6 billion (2013), $2.5 billion (2014), $2.4 billion (2015) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2016-02-25_0000927628-16-000140#main:ITEM7]; $3.127 billion (2019) on $424.765 billion purchase volume and a 4.51% net charge-off rate [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2020-02-20_0000927628-20-000102#main:ITEM7]; $1.4 billion (2020), $7.8 billion (2021), $4.9 billion (2022) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2023-02-24_0000927628-23-000117#main:ITEM7].
