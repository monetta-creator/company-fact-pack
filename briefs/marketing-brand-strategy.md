# Marketing and brand strategy

## Strategic Framing: Brand as a Governance and Growth Lever

Capital One's proxy disclosures treat "customer advocacy and brand" as one of the fixed performance factors under the "Winning with Our Customers and Associates" pillar of executive compensation, appearing continuously across at least four proxy cycles reviewed (2016 performance factors, 2022 performance factors, 2023 performance factors, and 2025 performance factors) [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2017-03-21_0001193125-17-090802#main] [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2023-03-22_0001193125-23-076700#main:ITEM6] [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2024-03-20_0001193125-24-072711#main:ITEM4] [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2026-03-25_0001193125-26-124131#main:ITEM4]. Net Promoter Score is the recurring proof point: the company reported record-high NPS levels in 2016 [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2017-03-21_0001193125-17-090802#main], NPS reaching "highest levels in the Company's history" in 2022 [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2023-03-22_0001193125-23-076700#main:ITEM6], and NPS "remained high across products, channels, and experiences" in both 2023 and 2025 performance narratives [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2024-03-20_0001193125-24-072711#main:ITEM4] [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2026-03-25_0001193125-26-124131#main:ITEM4]. In 2023 the company was ranked #1 in National Bank Overall Customer Satisfaction by J.D. Power for the fourth straight year [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2024-03-20_0001193125-24-072711#main:ITEM4]. The 2016 10-K frames this as a deliberate strategy: pursuing "franchise-enhancing customer relationships" that "create and sustain significant long-term value through long and loyal customer relationships," delivered via a "brand-defining" customer experience [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2016-02-25_0000927628-16-000140#main:ITEM7]. The 2017 proxy echoes this with "brand-defining software applications and features" as a stated 2016 achievement [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2017-03-21_0001193125-17-090802#main]. Brand is thus positioned in the packet not as a discrete marketing-department output but as an enterprise capability tied to the technology-transformation narrative and to executive incentive design.

## Marketing Expense Trajectory and Accounting Treatment

Reported marketing expense grew from $1.561 billion in FY2014 to $5.884 billion in FY2025 [src:edgar-cof/xbrl_companyfacts]. Year-by-year: FY2015 $1.744B, FY2016 $1.811B, FY2017 $1.670B, FY2018 $2.174B, FY2019 $2.274B, FY2020 $1.610B, FY2021 $2.871B, FY2022 $4.017B, FY2023 $4.009B, FY2024 $4.562B, FY2025 $5.884B [src:edgar-cof/xbrl_companyfacts]. Calculated against reported noninterest expense for the same years, marketing ran roughly 12.8%–13.4% of noninterest expense in 2014–2017, dropped to about 10.7% in the pandemic-affected FY2020 ($1.610B of $15.056B), then climbed to 17.3%–21.2% from FY2021 through FY2024, and sat at 19.3% in FY2025 ($5.884B of $30.498B noninterest expense) [src:edgar-cof/xbrl_companyfacts]. The FY2025 jump in both marketing spend and total noninterest expense coincides with the period in which the proxy describes "onboarding legacy Discover employees into Capital One systems" and references the "Discover Transaction" directly [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2026-03-25_0001193125-26-124131#main:ITEM4], though the packet does not itself state a closing date or attribute the expense increase explicitly to the merger. On accounting treatment, the FY2025 10-K defines marketing expense as covering "advertising (including through television, direct mail, digital, third-party partners and other channels), promotional materials and certain customer incentives, including spend-based bonuses," expensed as incurred rather than capitalized [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM8].

## Marketing Strategy Around the Discover Acquisition

Management commentary from 2024 merger-related communications gives the fullest picture of marketing intent in the packet. CEO Richard Fairbank described the deal's marketing synergy estimate as "pretty modest" — calculated as roughly 10% of the "billion dollars that Discover spends on marketing," attributed to combinable "mass customized marketing" and direct-marketing capabilities, but offset by "enhanced investment in the network and the brand of the network" [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-040125#main]. On brand positioning, Fairbank stated Discover has historically been "more of a mid-market kind of broad national focus" rather than "the very top of the market," but argued that advertising heavy-spender products still "provides lift for the whole company, because building an aspirational brand lifts all aspects of a company" [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-040125#main]. On post-close scale strategy, he framed the acquired network as a "rare unique asset" whose core constraint was scale, and positioned Capital One's card volume as the fix [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-040125#main]. On spend levels, guidance in a July 2024 call pointed to roughly $2.6 billion of marketing spend in the second half of that year, attributed to both deliberate growth investment and rising competitive intensity "at the top of the market" pushing up acquisition costs; Fairbank noted the second half "virtually every year" carries more marketing spend than the first half, consistent with the prior year's pattern [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-07-24_0000927628-24-000256#main]. Separately, an April 2024 call described the pending deal as creating a "Consumer Banking and global payments platform" with "powerful brands" and a franchise "of more than 100 million customers," and addressed antitrust positioning directly, noting the combined entity would still operate alongside three other networks post-close [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-04-26_0000927628-24-000164#main].

## Competitive Threats to Brand and Marketing Effectiveness

Both the FY2025 10-K and the 10-Q covering period ending 2025-06-30 carry near-identical risk-factor language: competitors "have developed, or may develop, substantially greater financial and other resources," may offer "richer value propositions," and "may use more effective advertising, marketing or cross-selling strategies to acquire and retain more customers, capture a greater share of spending and borrowings" and secure more attractive co-brand programs [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM1A] [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2025-07-31_0000927628-25-000247#main:ITEM1A]. Both filings warn that customer attrition combined with rate/fee concessions to retain customers "could reduce our revenues and therefore our earnings" [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM1A] [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2025-07-31_0000927628-25-000247#main:ITEM1A]. Post-acquisition, the 10-K adds that the Global Payment Network exposes the company to "substantial and increasingly intense competition" from both traditional payment networks and "new, emerging alternative payment providers," including direct competition for Network Partners issuing cards on the network [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM1A].

## Reputation and Community Commitments Tied to Brand

The 2026 proxy discloses an "historic $265 billion Community Benefits Plan in connection with the Discover Transaction," described as "the largest acquisition-related community commitment plan in the history of the U.S. banking system" [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2026-03-25_0001193125-26-124131#main:ITEM4]. The same disclosure reports Capital One ranked #36 on Fortune's 100 Best Companies to Work For list in 2025, its 14th consecutive year on the list [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2026-03-25_0001193125-26-124131#main:ITEM4] — down from #15 in the 2024 proxy (third consecutive year in the top 15, twelfth consecutive year overall) [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2024-03-20_0001193125-24-072711#main:ITEM4] and #10 in the 2023 proxy [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2023-03-22_0001193125-23-076700#main:ITEM6].
