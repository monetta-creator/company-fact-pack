# Credit cycle posture

## Credit Cycle Posture

### Underwriting Philosophy and Governance

Capital One's stated credit doctrine is built on the assumption that loans will face conditions worse than those prevailing at origination. Unsecured consumer underwriting "generally assume[s] that loans will be subject to an environment in which losses are higher than those prevailing at the time of underwriting," while commercial underwriting requires "strong cash flow, collateral, covenants and guarantees" [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2019-02-20_0000927628-19-000093#main:ITEM7]. Credit policy is organized around five standards — customer selection, underwriting, monitoring, remediation, and portfolio management — each backed by detailed procedures, with customer selection aimed at generating "above hurdle returns" [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2023-02-24_0000927628-23-000117#main:ITEM7]. Governance runs through the Chief Risk Officer together with Chief Consumer and Chief Commercial Credit Officers, who hold formal approval authority for credit decisions, with Division Presidents responsible for managing credit risk within their segments [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2019-02-20_0000927628-19-000093#main:ITEM7]. By FY2025, auto underwriting is described concretely as FICO-, LTV-, and credit-bureau-driven, and commercial underwriting continues to rely on borrower financial condition, industry assessment, collateral/guarantee evaluation, and an assigned risk rating with a monitoring schedule [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM7].

### Pre-Pandemic Cycle: 2015–2019

Credit metrics through the mid-cycle years show gradual normalization punctuated by idiosyncratic portfolio stress rather than broad deterioration. In FY2015, the net charge-off (NCO) rate rose 3 bps to 1.75% and 30+ day delinquency rose 9 bps to 3.00%, driven by taxi medallion and oil-and-gas exposure in Commercial Banking; the allowance for loan and lease losses grew $747 million to $5.1 billion, with coverage up 13 bps to 2.23% [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2016-02-25_0000927628-16-000140#main:ITEM7]. Consumer Banking's NCO rate ticked up 7 bps to 0.83% in Q2 2016 as auto loans grew as a share of the portfolio, even as 30+ day delinquency fell 60 bps to 4.07% [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2016-08-01_0000927628-16-000200#main:ITEM2]. Going into 2018, management expected Domestic Card charge-off pressure from loan seasoning to moderate while auto finance charge-offs were expected to rise gradually [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2018-02-21_0000927628-18-000107#main:ITEM7]. That played out in Q2 2018: NCO rate fell 25 bps to 2.42% against an elevated 2017 comparison base (oil-and-gas, taxi medallion), delinquency fell 43 bps to 3.05%, and the allowance declined $134 million to $7.4 billion as coverage rose to 3.12% [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2018-07-27_0000927628-18-000281#main:ITEM2]. Quarterly provision for credit losses stayed in a roughly $1.1–2.0 billion band from 2015 through 2019 [src:edgar-cof/xbrl_companyfacts], consistent with a benign, seasoning-driven cycle rather than a systemic credit event.

### COVID-19 Shock and CECL Transition

The pandemic produced the sharpest discontinuity in the packet. Q1 2020 CECL adoption combined with pandemic-driven allowance builds to double the coverage ratio to 5.4% from 2.7% at year-end 2019, driving a $1.3 billion net loss [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2020-05-01_0000927628-20-000173#main:ITEM2]; the allowance itself rose $6.9 billion to $14.1 billion in the quarter [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2020-05-01_0000927628-20-000173#main:ITEM1]. Builds continued into Q2 2020, taking the allowance to $16.8 billion (+$9.6 billion since year-end 2019) [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2020-08-06_0000927628-20-000242#main:ITEM1]. For full-year 2020, the company booked $5.0 billion in allowance builds across Q1–Q2 on expectations of economic worsening, ending the year with net income of $2.7 billion and a coverage ratio of 6.19%, up from 2.71% at year-end 2019 — the allowance itself rose $8.4 billion to $15.6 billion, reflecting both CECL adoption and pandemic-related builds [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM1][src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM8]. Quarterly provision spiked to $5.4 billion in 2020Q1 alone, versus a pre-pandemic quarterly norm near $1.3–1.7 billion [src:edgar-cof/xbrl_companyfacts]. The reversal came fast: by Q1 2021, government stimulus and strong credit performance cut the NCO rate 151 bps year-over-year to 1.21% and delinquency 63 bps to 1.98%, allowing the allowance to release $1.5 billion to $14.0 billion (coverage down 42 bps to 5.77%) [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2021-05-07_0000927628-21-000175#main:ITEM2]. Full-year 2021 NCO reached a historically low 0.88% [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2023-03-22_0001193125-23-076700#main:ITEM6].

### Post-Pandemic Normalization: 2022–2024

From 2022 onward, the packet documents a sustained, multi-year normalization cycle rather than a shock. FY2022 NCO rose to 1.36% (Domestic Card 2.5%), with provision swinging from a $1.9 billion benefit in 2021 to a $5.8 billion charge, alongside CET1 of 12.5% [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2023-03-22_0001193125-23-076700#main:ITEM6]. Q1 2023 continued this trend: NCO rose 110 bps year-over-year to 2.21% on "continued credit normalization," while the allowance grew $1.1 billion to $14.3 billion (coverage +40 bps to 4.64%) [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2023-05-05_0000927628-23-000168#main:ITEM2]. FY2023 NCO reached 2.7% (Domestic Card 4.6%), provision rose to $10.4 billion, and CET1 stood at 12.9% [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2024-03-20_0001193125-24-072711#main:ITEM4]. FY2024 NCO climbed further to 3.4% (Domestic Card 5.9%), provision to $11.7 billion, and CET1 to 13.5% [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2025-03-27_0001193125-25-065530#main:ITEM4]. XBRL allowance balances corroborate a steady build across this window, from $11.3 billion (2022-03-31) to $16.3 billion (2024-12-31) [src:edgar-cof/xbrl_companyfacts].

### Allowance Step-Change in 2025

XBRL data show the allowance for credit losses jumping from $15.9 billion (2025-03-31) to $23.9 billion (2025-06-30), then holding near $23.1–23.6 billion through 2026-03-31 [src:edgar-cof/xbrl_companyfacts]. This is a structural break in the series — roughly 50% larger than any prior quarter-end balance in the packet — but no MD&A, 10-K, or proxy excerpt in this packet explains the driver. It must be flagged, not interpreted.

### Liquidity Framework as Credit-Cycle Complement

Capital One frames liquidity risk as a companion discipline to credit risk: the Liquidity Adequacy Framework uses internal and regulatory stress testing to keep the balance sheet "resilient to uncertainties" from systemic or idiosyncratic events, backed by a Contingency Funding Plan and Recovery Plan [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2023-02-24_0000927628-23-000117#main:ITEM7]. CECL-era allowance mechanics extend to off-balance-sheet exposure: unfunded lending commitments in Commercial Banking are reserved using the same measurement objectives as funded loans, with builds/releases running through the provision line [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM8]. Partnership credit card arrangements further shape reported losses, since expected partner reimbursements are netted against the allowance and reduce reported net charge-offs and provision [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2023-02-24_0000927628-23-000117#main:ITEM8].
