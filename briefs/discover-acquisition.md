# The Discover acquisition

## Deal Structure and Mechanics

The transaction is a two-step merger executed under an Agreement and Plan of Merger dated February 19, 2024, among Discover, Capital One, and Vega Merger Sub, Inc. In the first merger, Merger Sub merges into Discover, with Discover surviving; immediately after, Discover merges into Capital One in a "second step merger," with Capital One as the surviving entity. Immediately following that, Discover Bank merges into Capital One, National Association, with Capital One, N.A. as the surviving bank [src:edgar-cof/S-4A_2025-01-03_0001193125-25-001580#main:preamble].

Consideration is 100% stock: Discover stockholders receive 1.0192 shares of Capital One common stock per Discover share (the "exchange ratio"). Based on Capital One's NYSE close on February 16, 2024 — the last trading day before announcement — that ratio represented approximately $139.86 per Discover share and aggregate merger consideration of approximately $35.3 billion. By the December 31, 2024 reference date used in the amended proxy statement, with Capital One at $178.32, the same ratio represented approximately $181.74 per Discover share [src:edgar-cof/S-4A_2025-01-03_0001193125-25-001580#main:preamble]. The deal materials characterize the exchange ratio as a 26.6% premium to Discover's share price under 100% stock consideration [src:edgar-dfs/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-039273#main]. Discover series C and series D preferred stock convert into new Capital One series O and series P preferred stock on terms "not materially less favorable"; each Discover depositary share becomes a Capital One depositary share on the same 1/100th interest basis. Outstanding Discover RSU awards convert into Capital One RSU awards, share counts adjusted by the exchange ratio [src:edgar-cof/S-4A_2024-07-26_0001193125-24-184991#main:PARTII].

Fairness-opinion work by PJT Partners produced implied exchange ratio ranges against the 1.0192x deal ratio: 0.7629x–1.1780x on a P/2024E EPS basis and 0.6723x–1.0361x on a P/TBVPS basis from comparable-company analysis [src:edgar-cof/S-4A_2025-01-03_0001193125-25-001580#main:PARTII], with a separate dividend-discount analysis producing its own implied range [src:edgar-cof/S-4_2024-04-19_0001193125-24-101304#main:PARTII] (the packet does not give the DDA range's numeric bounds).

## Strategic Rationale

Capital One frames the acquisition as advancing a "quest to build a global payments technology company," anchored in the Discover Global Network. The stated rationale is vertical integration: owning a network lets Capital One deal directly with merchants rather than through a network intermediary, capturing economics that otherwise accrue to an intermediary [src:edgar-dfs/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-040125#main]. The packet notes there are only two vertically integrated U.S.-based payments networks — American Express and Discover — competing against the larger Visa and Mastercard [src:edgar-dfs/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-040125#main]. Discover's network is described as a 40-year build: a credit card network started from scratch in the late 1980s, expanded by the 2005 PULSE acquisition (adding a national PIN debit network and roughly 4,000 member banks), and later the Diners Club acquisition for international reach [src:edgar-dfs/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-040125#main].

Scale figures cited for the combined network: 70 million merchant acceptance points across 200+ countries and territories, 1.8 million ATMs, 305 million+ global cardholders, and $550 billion+ in spend across the network [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-039259#main]. The materials also state Discover, despite this scale, remains the smallest of the four U.S.-based global payments networks, and that the acquisition adds scale and investment to make it more competitive [src:edgar-dfs/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-039259#main]. Discover CEO Michael Rhodes is quoted calling the deal an accelerant to growth that "maximizes value for our shareholders" [src:edgar-dfs/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-039259#main]. Separately, the packet notes Discover's intention (announced late 2023) to sell its student loan portfolio, with originations stopped February 1, 2024 .

## Projected Financial Metrics

Management's disclosed 2027E projections: operating expense synergy opportunity of $1.3 billion, marketing expense synergy of $0.1 billion, network synergy of $1.2 billion, adjusted EPS accretion of >15%, ROIC of 16%, and IRR of >20% (terminal value assuming 9.0x NTM P/E). Pro forma CET1 ratio at close is projected at ~14%, and 2023 pro forma insured deposits at 84% . These are management estimates tied to the assumptions stated in the filings' footnotes (non-GAAP adjusted EPS accretion, ROIC defined against Discover's synergized net income less excess capital) and should be read as projections, not realized results .

## Negotiation Timeline and Termination Terms

Discover's board first addressed inbound strategic inquiries at a September 22, 2023 meeting; a director subgroup (Maheras, Aronin, Case, Shepherd, Thierer) was formed to lead evaluation and met October 3, 2023 [src:edgar-cof/8-K_2025-02-10_0001193125-25-023100#main]. Between February 13–18, 2024, counsel exchanged merger agreement drafts: Wachtell Lipton's February 15 draft proposed a termination fee at 4.0% of transaction equity value; Sullivan & Cromwell's February 16 draft proposed 3.9%. The parties settled on $1.38 billion, ≈3.9% of the $35.3 billion aggregate merger consideration [src:edgar-dfs/425_2025-02-10_0001193125-25-023104#main]. That $1.38 billion fee is payable by either party under specified circumstances involving alternative acquisition proposals or a change in board recommendation [src:edgar-dfs/425_2024-02-22_0001193125-24-042828#main]. The agreement set a termination date 12 months after execution (i.e., February 19, 2025), automatically extendable three months (to May 19, 2025) if needed to obtain regulatory approvals [src:edgar-cof/S-4A_2025-01-03_0001193125-25-001580#main:PARTII].

## Closing Conditions and Regulatory Path

Completion requires: Discover and Capital One stockholder approvals; NYSE listing authorization for the new shares; required regulatory approvals, including the Federal Reserve Board and the OCC; effectiveness of the Form S-4; and the absence of injunctions or legal restraints [src:edgar-dfs/425_2024-02-22_0001193125-24-042828#main]. Each party's obligation is further conditioned on the other's representations and warranties being accurate, material performance of obligations, and receipt of a tax opinion that the mergers qualify as a reorganization under Internal Revenue Code Section 368(a) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2025-02-20_0000927628-25-000092#main:ITEM1A]. The Federal Reserve review proceeds under BHC Act Sections 3 and 4(c)(8), weighing financial and managerial resources, capital adequacy, liquidity, and management competence and integrity [src:edgar-cof/S-4_2024-04-19_0001193125-24-101304#main:PARTII]. Discover's own risk disclosures flag that regulators could impose conditions, limitations, or restrictions that delay closing, add costs, or reduce anticipated benefits, with no assurance any such conditions would be avoided [src:edgar-dfs/10-KA_2024-12-23_0001393612-24-000073#main:ITEM1A]. Capital One's 10-K separately notes it expects to incur substantial non-recurring transaction and integration expenses, which "may be greater than anticipated due to unexpected events," and flags exposure to lawsuits challenging the transaction [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2025-02-20_0000927628-25-000092#main:ITEM1A].
