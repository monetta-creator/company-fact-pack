# The 2019 data breach and aftermath

## The 2019 data breach and aftermath

### The incident

On March 22–23, 2019, an outside individual exploited a configuration vulnerability to gain unauthorized access to Capital One's systems, obtaining personal information belonging to people who had applied for its credit card products and to existing credit card customers [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM1]. Capital One did not disclose the breach publicly until July 29, 2019 [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2025-11-03_0000927628-25-000276#main:ITEM1]. Management reported the event to the Board on July 19, 2019, ten days before public disclosure [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2020-03-19_0001193125-20-079122#main:ITEM5]. The company states it "immediately fixed the configuration vulnerability" and retained an outside cybersecurity firm that confirmed the fix, verifying no other instances existed in its environment [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2020-03-19_0001193125-20-079122#main:ITEM5] [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM1]. The individual responsible was arrested by the FBI on July 29, 2019, and federal prosecution followed; the U.S. Attorney's Office stated it believed the data had been recovered with no evidence of fraud or further sharing [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2020-03-19_0001193125-20-079122#main:ITEM5].

### Board and governance response

Following discovery, independent members of the Risk Committee, Audit Committee, Governance and Nominating Committee, and the full Board — including directors with cybersecurity and technology experience — met over 20 times to review the incident and the company's response, supplemented by informal update sessions with management [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2020-03-19_0001193125-20-079122#main:ITEM5]. The Board also engaged independent third-party experts for ongoing incident-response and root-cause reporting [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2020-03-19_0001193125-20-079122#main:ITEM5]. The packet does not provide detail beyond this summary on what governance or reporting-line changes resulted (see Gaps).

### Financial cost

Cumulative incremental expenses tied to remediation and response reached $138 million through year-end 2020, offset by $73 million in insurance recoveries; $66 million of that total was incurred during 2020 alone, offset by $39 million in insurance recoveries that year [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM1]. These costs "mainly consist of customer notifications, credit monitoring, technology costs, and professional and legal support" [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM1]. Capital One carries cyber risk insurance with a $400 million coverage limit and a $10 million deductible, met in the third quarter of 2019 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM1]. As of the FY2020 10-K, the company expected any further net expenses in future periods to be immaterial and stated it did not believe the incident would materially impact its strategy or long-term financial health [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM1]. The packet contains no cost figures after FY2020 (see Gaps).

### Regulatory resolution

In August 2020, Capital One entered consent orders with the Federal Reserve and the OCC arising from regulatory reviews of the incident and tied to ongoing cybersecurity and operational risk management enhancements [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM8]. It paid an $80 million penalty to the U.S. Treasury under the OCC agreement; the Federal Reserve agreement carried no monetary penalty [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM8]. The OCC terminated its consent order on August 31, 2022 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2023-02-24_0000927628-23-000117#main:ITEM1A] [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2023-05-05_0000927628-23-000168#main:ITEM1]. The packet does not indicate whether the Federal Reserve order has since been lifted (see Gaps). Separately, the company received inquiries from Congress, federal regulators, relevant Canadian regulators, the Department of Justice, and roughly fourteen state Attorneys General regarding the incident, and stated it was cooperating with these offices [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM8].

### Litigation

Consumer and securities litigation followed the disclosure. As of the Q1/Q2 2020 10-Qs, Capital One had been named in approximately 73 putative consumer class actions (61 in U.S. federal courts, 12 in Canadian courts), consolidated for pretrial proceedings before a multi-district litigation panel in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia, Alexandria Division [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2020-05-01_0000927628-20-000173#main:ITEM1] [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2020-08-06_0000927628-20-000242#main:ITEM1]. A parallel securities class action named the company and certain officers, alleging violations of federal securities laws tied to statements and omissions about information security practices, with a proposed class period of purchasers/acquirers of Capital One securities from July 23, 2015 to July 29, 2019 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM8].

By mid-2025, the U.S. consumer and securities claims are no longer discussed in the packet's Cybersecurity Incident disclosures; only the Canadian track remains active. As of the Q2 and Q3 2025 10-Qs, Capital One is named in 4 putative consumer class actions in Canadian courts [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2025-07-31_0000927628-25-000247#main:ITEM1] [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2025-11-03_0000927628-25-000276#main:ITEM1]. Procedurally: in Q2 2022 a British Columbia trial court preliminarily certified a class of impacted Canadian consumers excluding Quebec; that certification was appealed, and on July 4, 2024 the British Columbia Court of Appeal denied both parties' appeals [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2025-11-03_0000927628-25-000276#main:ITEM1]. In Q3 2023 a Quebec trial court preliminarily authorized a class of impacted Quebec consumers; on appeal, the Quebec Court of Appeal affirmed the trial court's ruling on February 25, 2025 [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2025-11-03_0000927628-25-000276#main:ITEM1]. The remaining two Canadian actions, both pending in Alberta, remain at a preliminary stage [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2025-11-03_0000927628-25-000276#main:ITEM1]. The packet gives no outcome, dismissal, or settlement figure for the U.S. MDL consumer or securities cases, nor for the Canadian actions (see Gaps).

### Framing in current risk disclosure

Capital One's FY2021 and FY2022 10-Ks continue to cite the 2019 incident as a live risk-factor example, noting it "resulted in fines, litigation, government investigations and other regulatory enforcement inquiries, as well as consent orders" [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2022-02-25_0000927628-22-000106#main:ITEM1A], with the FY2022 filing adding that consent orders were specifically with "the Federal Reserve and the OCC" and noting the OCC's August 2022 termination [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2023-02-24_0000927628-23-000117#main:ITEM1A]. A January 2025 Form 425 discusses an aggregate range of reasonably possible losses (up to $140 million as of June 30, 2024) for legal and regulatory matters generally, but does not attribute this figure specifically to the 2019 incident as opposed to other proceedings such as the card product misclassification matter [src:edgar-cof/425_2025-01-24_0001193125-25-011795#main].
