# Partnership history

## Partnership history

### Portfolio acquisitions that built the co-brand book (2015–2019)

Capital One's retail partnership card business grew mainly through acquisition. On December 1, 2015, it closed the acquisition of General Electric Capital Corporation's Healthcare Financial Services business, adding roughly $9.2 billion in assets, including $8.2 billion of loans [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2018-02-21_0000927628-18-000107#main:ITEM1]. On September 25, 2017, it acquired credit card assets and liabilities of World's Foremost Bank — Cabela's Incorporated's bank subsidiary — from Synovus Bank, adding approximately $5.7 billion to the domestic credit card loans held-for-investment portfolio [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2018-02-21_0000927628-18-000107#main:ITEM1], a figure repeated in the FY2018 10-K [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2019-02-20_0000927628-19-000093#main:ITEM1]. On October 5, 2018, Capital One completed the Bass Pro cobrand credit card portfolio acquisition, adding approximately $534 million to the same portfolio [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2019-02-20_0000927628-19-000093#main:ITEM1].

### The Walmart program: contract, portfolio purchase, launch

The Walmart relationship split the card-issuance contract from the receivables purchase into two separate steps. On July 26, 2018, Capital One announced a new long-term credit card program agreement making it the exclusive issuer of Walmart's cobrand and private-label credit card program in the U.S. [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2019-02-20_0000927628-19-000093#main:ITEM1]. On January 22, 2019, it announced a definitive agreement to acquire the existing Walmart receivables portfolio, expected at closing to be approximately $9 billion [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2019-02-20_0000927628-19-000093#main:ITEM1]. The program launched on September 24, 2019, making Capital One the exclusive issuer of Walmart's cobrand and private-label card program [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2020-02-20_0000927628-20-000102#main:ITEM1] [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM1]. The portfolio acquisition closed October 11, 2019, adding approximately $8.1 billion to domestic credit card loans held for investment — below the ~$9 billion estimated at announcement [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2020-02-20_0000927628-20-000102#main:ITEM1] [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM1].

### Walmart card economics over time

CFPB-filed card agreements show pricing on the Walmart Rewards product moving upward across four snapshots. As of December 31, 2020: 0% introductory APR for the first 9 months, non-introductory purchase/cash-advance rates of 17.99%–26.99%, transfer APR 17.99%–26.99%, cash-advance APR 26.99%, no annual fee, 3% transfer fee, late fees up to $39 [src:cfpb-agreements/2020_Q4#ex_Capital_One_Credit_Card_Agreement_for_Capital_One_Walmart_Re]. The same filing describes a separately-detailed rewards program and promotional "Segments," including a waived-interest offer with no interest charges on purchases for a specified period [src:cfpb-agreements/2020_Q4#ex_Capital_One_Credit_Card_Agreement_for_Capital_One_Walmart_Re]. As of September 30, 2021, the flat purchase/quick-cash APR was 26.99%, same ≥25-day grace period, no annual fee, late fee up to $39 [src:cfpb-agreements/2021_Q4#ex_CAPITAL_ONE_NATIONAL_ASSOCIATION_Credit_Card_Agreement_for_W]. As of March 31, 2023, the range widened: 0% intro APR for 9 months, non-intro rates 17.99%–28.99%, transfer APR 17.99%–28.99%, cash-advance APR 28.99% [src:cfpb-agreements/2023_Q4#ex_CAPITAL_ONE_NATIONAL_ASSOCIATION_Credit_Card_Agreement_for_C]. A later filing using the same March 31, 2023 effective date lists a flat 28.99% purchase/quick-cash APR [src:cfpb-agreements/2024_Q1#ex_CAPITAL_ONE_NATIONAL_ASSOCIATION_Credit_Card_Agreement_for_W]. The product name itself shifts between "Capital One Walmart Rewards® Cards" (2020, 2023 Q4 filings) and "Walmart Rewards® Cards" (2021 Q4, 2024 Q1 filings) [src:cfpb-agreements/2020_Q4#ex_Capital_One_Credit_Card_Agreement_for_Capital_One_Walmart_Re] [src:cfpb-agreements/2023_Q4#ex_CAPITAL_ONE_NATIONAL_ASSOCIATION_Credit_Card_Agreement_for_C] [src:cfpb-agreements/2021_Q4#ex_CAPITAL_ONE_NATIONAL_ASSOCIATION_Credit_Card_Agreement_for_W] [src:cfpb-agreements/2024_Q1#ex_CAPITAL_ONE_NATIONAL_ASSOCIATION_Credit_Card_Agreement_for_W]; the packet does not explain the change.

### Partnership exits and portfolio sales (2019–2022)

Alongside acquisitions, Capital One periodically shed partnership relationships. In Q2 2019, it decided to exit "several small partnership portfolios" in Credit Card, selling approximately $900 million of receivables and transferring approximately $100 million to held-for-sale as of June 30, 2019, producing a $49 million gain on sale in other non-interest income and a $68 million allowance release [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2020-02-20_0000927628-20-000102#main:ITEM1]. The same figures recur in the Q1 and Q2 2020 10-Qs [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2020-05-01_0000927628-20-000173#main:ITEM2] [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2020-08-06_0000927628-20-000242#main:ITEM2]. None of these filings name the exited partners. In Q4 2020, Capital One agreed to sell a partnership credit card loan portfolio of approximately $2.1 billion — transferred to held-for-sale as of September 30, 2020 — resulting in a $327 million allowance release [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2021-02-25_0000927628-21-000094#main:ITEM1]; again no counterparty is named. The Q1 2022 earnings release reports a further "gain on sale of partnership card portfolios" of $192 million pre-tax, $0.35 diluted EPS impact [src:edgar-cof/8-K_2022-04-26_0000927628-22-000152#ex_ex991q12022earningsrelease.htm], with no partner identified and no portfolio size given.
