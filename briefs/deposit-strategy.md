# Deposit strategy

## Deposit Strategy Doctrine Brief

## Funding Philosophy: Deposits as the Primary, Lower-Cost Source

Capital One's stated funding philosophy across multiple reporting periods is consistent: insured retail deposits are the primary funding source because they are relatively stable and lower-cost compared to alternatives. Wholesale funding — senior and subordinated notes, securitized debt obligations, federal funds purchased, repo agreements, and FHLB advances — is explicitly secondary and supplemental [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM7] [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2025-02-20_0000927628-25-000092#main:ITEM7] [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2024-08-01_0000927628-24-000264#main:ITEM2] [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2024-10-31_0000927628-24-000306#main:ITEM2]. This framing is repeated verbatim across FY2023 and FY2024 10-Ks and two 2024 10-Qs, indicating it is a durable balance-sheet posture rather than a one-period statement.

The demand for that funding is tied to loan book growth, particularly credit cards: loans held for investment rose $8.1B to $320.5B in FY2023 and $7.3B to $327.8B in FY2024, both attributed primarily to credit card loan growth [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2024-02-23_0000927628-24-000094#main:ITEM7] [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2025-02-20_0000927628-25-000092#main:ITEM7]. Interim 2024 quarters show loans held roughly flat to slightly down ($320.2B at 9/30/2024, $318.2B at 6/30/2024) as commercial paydowns offset card and auto growth [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2024-10-31_0000927628-24-000306#main:ITEM2] [src:edgar-cof/10-Q_2024-08-01_0000927628-24-000264#main:ITEM2].

## The Discover Acquisition as a Deposit-Scale Event

Deposit strategy is inseparable from the Discover transaction. Pre-close investor materials from February–March 2024 framed Discover's direct-to-consumer deposit base as "$84 billion in stable, largely insured consumer deposits, the most sought-after funding source in banking" [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-040125#main] [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-03-06_0000927628-24-000114#main]. The deal was also pitched as a scale move: combined 2023 total deposits (Capital One standalone) ranked #26 among reporting bank holding companies per Federal Reserve Y-9C data, and management framed the acquisition as necessary to compete with the nation's largest banks [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-039259#main].

The transaction closed May 18, 2025 [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2026-03-25_0001193125-26-124131#main:ITEM4]. At close, it contributed $106.9 billion of deposits — meaningfully above the $84B figure cited in the pre-close deal pitch, indicating either deposit growth at Discover between announcement and close or a difference in what was being measured (total vs. direct-to-consumer) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM7] [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-040125#main]. The packet does not reconcile these two figures.

## Distribution Network and Customer Value Proposition

Capital One's deposit-gathering model rests on a hybrid digital/physical footprint: no-fee national bank branding paired with a small physical network. As of early 2024, materials cite 259 branches, 55 Cafés in 21 of the top 25 MSAs, 16,000+ cash deposit locations, and 80,000+ fee-free ATMs [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-039259#main]. A same-day earnings-call filing instead states 250 branches, 80,000 fee-free ATMs, and 16,000 cash deposit locations [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-040125#main] — the branch count differs from the investor-deck figure filed the same day, and the packet does not explain the gap. By July 2024, a CRA/community filing cites branches in the DC, New York, and Gulf Coast metro footprints, Cafés in 21 of the top 25 MSAs, 16,000+ cash deposit locations, and "more than 70,000" fee-free ATMs [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-07-17_0001193125-24-180490#main] — a lower ATM figure than the 80,000+ cited five months earlier, again unreconciled in the packet.

The customer value proposition centers on fee elimination: no fees, no minimums, no overdraft fees, with Capital One repeatedly described as the only major bank offering free overdraft protection without minimum balance requirements [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-07-17_0001193125-24-180490#main]. Since eliminating overdraft fees in 2021, customers are stated to have saved over $500 million in potential fees [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-07-17_0001193125-24-180490#main]. The bank has ranked #1 in J.D. Power's U.S. National Banking Overall Customer Satisfaction study for four years running (2020–2023) [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-02-20_0001193125-24-039259#main]. Stated forward commitments include growing Bank On–certified checking accounts (360 Checking) by at least 50% over the then-current base, and enabling access for roughly 100,000 ITIN-holding consumers through enhanced digital acceptance [src:edgar-cof/425_2024-07-17_0001193125-24-180490#main].

## Deposit Growth Trajectory and Mix

Reported year-end totals show steady organic growth followed by a step-change at the Discover close. Total deposits rose $14.3B to $362.7B at year-end 2024 from year-end 2023, attributed to the "national consumer banking strategy," partially offset by maturing brokered deposits (which fell to $11.6B from $18.5B) [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2025-02-20_0000927628-25-000092#main:ITEM7]. Estimated uninsured deposits were $64.9B (18% of total) at year-end 2024, essentially flat in share versus $64.2B (18%) at year-end 2023 [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2025-02-20_0000927628-25-000092#main:ITEM7].

At year-end 2025, total deposits reached $475.8B, up $113.1B from year-end 2024, "primarily driven by the Transaction and continued growth from our national banking strategy," with the Transaction alone contributing $106.9B as of the closing date [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM7]. Brokered deposits rose to $19.2B (from $11.6B), and estimated uninsured deposits rose in dollar terms to $71.9B but fell as a share of the larger base, to 15% from 18% [src:edgar-cof/10-K_2026-02-19_0000927628-26-000024#main:ITEM7]. Quarterly XBRL figures corroborate the timing: total deposits held near $367.5B at 2025-03-31, then jumped to $468.1B at 2025-06-30 — consistent with the May 18, 2025 close — and reached $489.1B by 2026-03-31 [src:edgar-cof/xbrl_companyfacts]. The 2026 proxy statement credits 2025 performance to closing the Transaction, "on track to deliver expected deal synergies," and growth in "national digital banking franchise... checking accounts and core deposits," backed by $5.9B in marketing spend [src:edgar-cof/DEF14A_2026-03-25_0001193125-26-124131#main:ITEM4].

## Gaps

The packet has several limitations worth flagging rather than papering over. First, footprint figures (branches, ATMs) are inconsistent across filings within and across dates — 259 vs. 250 branches, 80,000+ vs. 70,000+ ATMs — with no source in the packet explaining the discrepancy; any brief consumer should treat these as approximate, not reconciled. Second, the $84B Discover deposit estimate cited pre-close does not match the $106.9B actually contributed at closing, and no source explains the delta. Third, the Events list for 2025–2026 consists almost entirely of 8-K filings tagged "other" with no extracted substantive content, so no deposit-specific disclosures (e.g., quarterly deposit cost/rate commentary, deposit beta, or post-merger integration deposit attrition/retention) could be drawn from them. Fourth, the packet contains no data on deposit pricing, cost of deposits, deposit mix by product (savings vs. checking vs. CDs), or competitive deposit-rate positioning — all of which would normally sit inside a "deposit strategy" brief but are simply absent from this source set.
