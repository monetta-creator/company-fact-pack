Four rules

  1. Public sources onlySEC filings, regulator data, court records, the CFPB's databases. Every document carries its URL, retrieval date, and cryptographic hash.
  2. Every claim is traceableSentence → citation → excerpt → provenance record → public source. A citation that can't complete that chain fails the build.
  3. Numbers come from one placeA governed metric store with one definition per figure — never scraped from prose, where the basis is unknowable.
  4. People approve, models proposeMachine-written analysis enters as a draft on a review branch. A human merges it, or it never counts.

What's inside

A decade of 10-K, 10-Q, and 8-K filings from Capital One and Discover with their exhibits; monthly credit-card trust performance; FDIC bank financials and branch deposits; ten years of credit-card agreements; the CFPB complaint database; Federal Reserve stress tests; enforcement orders; litigation records. Structured into entities, events, metrics, and doctrine briefs — compiled into one searchable database.
